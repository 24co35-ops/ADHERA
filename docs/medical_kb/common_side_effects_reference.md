# Common Medication Side Effects & Clinical Guidance Reference

## 1. Overview of Common Medications & Expected Side Effects

### Metformin (Biguanide for Type 2 Diabetes)
- **Common Side Effects**: Mild stomach upset, nausea, diarrhea, metallic taste, loss of appetite.
- **Minimization Strategy**: Taking with or immediately after meals significantly reduces gastrointestinal symptoms. Extended-release formulations often improve tolerance.
- **Red Flag**: Severe fatigue, muscle pain, difficulty breathing, or stomach discomfort (rare risk of lactic acidosis — requires immediate emergency care).

### Lisinopril / ACE Inhibitors (Hypertension & Heart Failure)
- **Common Side Effects**: Persistent dry cough, mild dizziness, lightheadedness when standing up quickly.
- **Minimization Strategy**: Stand up slowly from sitting or lying positions. If dry cough persists, consult provider for ARB alternatives (e.g., Losartan).
- **Red Flag**: Swelling of the face, lips, tongue, or throat (angioedema — seek immediate emergency care).

### Amlodipine (Calcium Channel Blocker)
- **Common Side Effects**: Peripheral edema (swelling of ankles/feet), facial flushing, dizziness, headache.
- **Minimization Strategy**: Elevate feet while resting. Report persistent swelling to your provider.

### Atorvastatin / Statins (Lipid Lowering)
- **Common Side Effects**: Mild headache, digestive symptoms, mild joint soreness.
- **Red Flag**: Unexplained muscle pain, tenderness, or weakness accompanied by dark urine (rhabdomyolysis — stop medication and contact provider immediately).

### Levothyroxine (Thyroid Hormone Replacement)
- **Common Side Effects**: Heart palpitations, tremors, insomnia, weight loss if dose is too high; fatigue and weight gain if dose is too low.
- **Guidance**: Must be taken on an empty stomach with plain water at least 30–60 minutes before morning coffee, tea, or food.

## 2. Side Effect Severity Grading in ADHERA
- **Level 1 (Mild)**: Noticeable but does not disrupt daily routine.
- **Level 2 (Moderate)**: Uncomfortable and slightly impairs daily activity.
- **Level 3 (High)**: Significantly affects health or ability to function; provider notified.
- **Level 4 (Emergency)**: Acute life-threatening symptoms (chest pain, shortness of breath, anaphylaxis); requires emergency medical attention (911 / 112).
